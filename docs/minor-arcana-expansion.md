# Minor Arcana Expansion — Design Framework

Version: 0.1 (extends `tarot-aviary-art-direction.md` and the v0.2 bird map)

This document expands Tarot Aviary from the 22 Major Arcana into the full 56-card
Minor Arcana, plus a menu of further expansion directions. It keeps every existing
rule (anatomically bird, streetwear-fitted, high-res pixel art, urban-fantasy tarot
frame) and reuses the v0.2 variety levers (`silhouetteClass`, `poseEnergy`,
`accent`). All Major Arcana species stay reserved — the Minor Arcana introduces a
fresh cast so the two halves never blur.

---

## 1. The core novelty: four suits = four city crews

The Majors are legendary individuals. The Minors are the **four elemental crews**
that make up the bird city — guilds with a habitat, a palette, a mood, and a small
recurring cast shown across all 14 cards in different scenes. This gives instant
suit-readability (you know the suit before you read the title) and lets pip cards
tell stories instead of just stacking symbols.

| Suit | Element | Crew name | Domain | Habitat / time | Palette | Recurring cast (none reused from Majors) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wands** | Fire | **Emberwings** | drive, passion, creativity, hustle, performance | rooftops & neon, high summer noon | ember orange, hot magenta, gold, ash charcoal | Anna's Hummingbird, Vermilion Flycatcher, Greater Bird-of-Paradise, Common Swift, American Kestrel |
| **Cups** | Water | **Tidewatch** | emotion, love, intuition, memory, bonds | canals, rain, harbor, fountains, dusk | teal, rain gray, pearl, harbor blue, soft rose | Great Blue Heron, Roseate Spoonbill, Hooded Merganser, Sanderling, Wood Duck |
| **Swords** | Air | **Cutting Wind** | intellect, conflict, truth, words, strategy | glass spires, antennas, storm wind, night | steel, ink black, glass blue, white, cold cyan | Loggerhead Shrike, Blue Jay, Northern Goshawk, Black Drongo, Merlin |
| **Pentacles** | Earth | **Groundwork** | money, craft, work, home, the material world | markets, alleys, construction, nests, morning | brass, copper, soil brown, moss, coin gray | Satin Bowerbird, Sociable Weaver, Rock Pigeon, European Starling, Hoopoe |

Why these casts are deliberate:
- **Wands / hummingbird + swift + bird-of-paradise** = pure motion, heat, and display.
- **Cups / heron + spoonbill + merganser** = patience, tenderness, and courtship at the water.
- **Swords / shrike ("the butcher bird") + jay + goshawk** = the deck's intellect-and-conflict suit; the shrike's real impaling behavior carries Swords' edge *without gore* (symbolic thorns/blade-feathers, never wounds).
- **Pentacles / bowerbird + weaver + pigeon** = the bowerbird literally builds and hoards treasure, the weaver builds communal homes, the pigeon is the city's living currency. Wealth and craft, made avian.

## 2. Court cards — renamed for the bird world (optional)

The contract allows renaming "unless later renamed by the game." Pages/Knights/
Queens/Kings read human; I recommend guild-role names that stay avian:

| Classic | Renamed | Role | Pose energy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Page | **Fledgling** | apprentice / messenger; the suit's youngest hero species | active, curious |
| Knight | **Outrider** | the mobile actor; always in flight or motion | dynamic |
| Queen | **Matron** | inner mastery of the element; commanding, nurturing | static, poised |
| King | **Elder** | full external authority of the element; the crew boss | static, monumental |

Each court is the suit's flagship species (e.g., the Heron is the Elder of Cups).
If you'd rather keep classic names for tarot legibility, the framework works
unchanged — only the title text differs.

## 3. Pip method — scenes, not symbol stacks

Each Ace–Ten depicts the suit's recurring cast in a **behavioral vignette** matching
the traditional (Rider-Waite-Smith) pip meaning, translated into bird behavior.
Fewer hero species, more storytelling — this is the main lever against the
"40 pips feel repetitive" problem. The suit number is read from staged props
(coin-berries, blade-feathers, lit wands, brimming vessels), not from a literal
count of identical icons.

Dark-card rules carry over: Swords stays symbolic — thorns, broken antennas,
blade-shaped feathers, storm — **never wounds, blood, or nooses.** The 10 of Swords
("rock bottom") resolves with a sunrise behind it = renewal, not death.

## 4. Suit-level art coding (instant readability)

Bake a fixed visual signature into every card of a suit so the deck reads at a glance:

- **Border motif:** Wands = flame/ember filigree · Cups = wave/droplet filigree · Swords = wind/blade filigree · Pentacles = gear/coin/vine filigree.
- **Time of day:** Wands = blazing noon · Cups = rainy dusk · Swords = storm night · Pentacles = market morning.
- **Suit emblem** in the title cartouche (small crest per crew).
- **Accent discipline:** each card still carries one distinct `accent` hue (from the v0.2 system) so cards within a suit don't flatten into one color block.

## 5. Other ways to expand the set

Ranked by value-to-effort for a card game:

1. **"Aviary Arcana" — game-original trumps beyond the 22.** Expansion tarot decks
   often add cards. Strong bird-native candidates: *The Migration, The Roost,
   The Storm, The Molt, The Flock, The Eclipse.* High lore + mechanical value.
2. **Suit crests + card backs + deck emblem.** Four crew crests and a unified back —
   cheap, high-impact, needed for any real deck.
3. **Reversals as "Molt" alt-art.** Tarot reversals already carry meaning; render a
   shadow/iridescent "molting" variant of any card for the reversed state. Doubles
   art value and feeds a chase/collectible layer.
4. **Realm / District location cards (4–8).** The four crew habitats (Neon Rooftops,
   The Canals, The Spires, The Market) as zone/board cards — directly supports the
   Phaser game layer hinted at in `skills/`.
5. **Fledgling origin cards for the 22 Majors.** Younger versions of the legends —
   lore depth, and a natural "prequel" or promo set.
6. **Token / counter art tied to mechanics.** Feather, Egg, Nest, Coin, Tear, Storm,
   Ember — the resources the gameplayFantasy fields keep implying.
7. **Chase / iridescent variants.** Holographic "iridescent molt," night-mode,
   first-edition foils — collectibility without new designs.
8. **A rare 5th suit (Aether / Feathers).** If the game wants a wildcard element —
   migratory/ghost birds (e.g., the Arctic Tern's wider flock) bridging Major and
   Minor. Optional, only if mechanics call for it.

## 6. Review additions (on top of the existing checklist)

- Is the suit identifiable before reading the title (cast + palette + border + time of day)?
- Does the pip scene clearly read its traditional meaning through bird behavior?
- Is the Minor cast kept free of all 22 Major species?
- For Swords: symbolic only — no wounds, blood, ropes, or nooses?
- Does each card still carry one distinct accent hue within its suit?
