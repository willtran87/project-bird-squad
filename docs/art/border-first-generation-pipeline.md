# Border-First Minor Arcana Generation Pipeline

This document defines the preferred production workflow for regenerating Bird
Squad Minor Arcana cards. It exists to prevent border drift, title drift,
forced pip counts, anatomy failures, suit bleed, and inconsistent final card
dimensions.

The visual source of truth remains `docs/art/art-bible.md`. This workflow is an
execution layer for the current art bible rules, especially the accepted Tarot
Aviary border, fixed 1024x1536 canvas, modern bird-city streetwear, anatomical
bird characters, natural pip integration, and Plumes as biological resonance.

## Goals

- Keep every Minor Arcana card in the same accepted Tarot Aviary deck frame.
- Render titles deterministically as `Ace of Plumes`, `Two of Basins`,
  `Fledgling of Quills`, and so on.
- Generate only the central illustration area whenever possible.
- Make pip counts feel native to the scene rather than pasted onto the card.
- Preserve bird anatomy: exactly one named species, natural bird body, exactly
  two wings, exactly two legs, no hands, no arms, no wing-hands, and no extra
  limbs.
- Preserve the current fashion direction: modern urban streetwear with
  individual character, not gang uniforms, fantasy costumes, or sci-fi armor.

## Layer Model

Finished Minor Arcana cards should be treated as layered assets:

```text
1024x1536 base canvas
+ generated center illustration
+ locked per-suit border overlay
+ deterministic title text
+ optional deterministic suit or rank inset details
= final card PNG
```

The image model should not be responsible for inventing the frame, setting the
final title typography, or maintaining exact title spelling. Those are
production-system responsibilities.

The image model is responsible for keeping all important art inside the future
overlay-safe center. Do not accept a center-art candidate if the bird's head,
beak, feet, tail, held prop, suit action, or any countable pip element sits near
the outer edge, top medallion zone, side rail zone, or bottom title cartouche
zone. Pull subjects and pips inward during generation instead of trying to save
cropped art during compositing.

## Locked Border Overlays

Create reusable border geometry per suit. The preferred production path is a
polished imagegen-created transparent raster template, with the procedural SVG
overlay kept as a fallback and inspection aid.

| Suit | Overlay |
| --- | --- |
| Plumes | Shared Tarot Aviary frame with Plumes inset motifs; deterministic title rendered by compositor |
| Basins | Shared Tarot Aviary frame with Basins inset motifs; deterministic title rendered by compositor |
| Quills | Shared Tarot Aviary frame with Quills inset motifs; deterministic title rendered by compositor |
| Nests | Shared Tarot Aviary frame with Nests inset motifs; deterministic title rendered by compositor |

All overlays must share the same geometry:

- 1024 px wide by 1536 px tall.
- Same dark outer keyline.
- Same gilded inner filigree rail.
- Same corner medallion size, position, and shape.
- Same slim side rail width and placement.
- Same centered top medallion position.
- Same central image window.
- Same bottom title cartouche width, height, baseline, and placement.

Only the suit-specific inset motifs should differ. A card-specific object,
wing, prop, light, weather effect, plume, quill, basin, shine tag, or piece of
scenery must never cover, replace, crop, distort, redraw, or break through the
locked border.

Template source files live under
`assets/templates/minor-arcana/raster-border-templates/`. Use
`docs/art/tarot-border-template-pipeline.md` and
`docs/art/prompt-packs/tarot-border-template-prompts.md` to create and prepare
those assets.

Fallback repo-owned SVG overlays are generated by
`tools/generate-minor-card-overlays.mjs` into
`assets/templates/minor-arcana/card-overlays/{suit}/{cardId}.svg`, with a
manifest at `assets/templates/minor-arcana/card-overlays/overlay-manifest.json`.

Reject any generated or composited card with a border that reads as a different
deck: simplified cream border, lighter frame, altered cartouche, missing
medallions, cropped rails, changed frame thickness, or newly invented ornament.

## Deterministic Title Text

Do not rely on generated text for final title quality. Render the title after
art generation into the locked bottom cartouche.

Title rules:

- Minor Arcana titles use rank and suit: `Ace of Plumes`, `Nine of Quills`,
  `Matron of Nests`, etc.
- Traditional tarot suit names should not appear in final card text.
- Game/internal names may guide prompts and filenames, but should not replace
  the formal card title in the cartouche.
- Clothing, patches, caps, pouches, bags, and background signage should not
  contain readable slogans, logos, crew names, brand marks, or pseudo-writing.

## Center-Art Generation

Generate the center illustration as the expressive layer. Prompts should focus
on:

- the card's named bird species;
- natural avian anatomy and species-specific proportions;
- modern bird-city fashion fitted to bird bodies;
- the suit's environment, material language, and behavior;
- the card meaning translated into bird action;
- exact pip requirements for Ace through Ten;
- a composition that leaves safe room for the locked border and title.

Prompts should avoid asking the model to solve the final frame or typography.
If a generator cannot produce a borderless center image, use the locked border
language as a style reference during generation, then composite the accepted
overlay on top and reject cards where generated border remnants fight the
overlay.

The accepted medium is high-resolution pixel art. Reject realistic painting,
semi-realistic illustration, smooth digital painting, photographic rendering,
soft airbrush realism, or non-pixel-art concept art before compositing, even if
the species, suit, and pip counts are otherwise correct.

## Natural Pip Planning

Each Ace through Ten needs a pip plan before generation. The pip plan should
define how the exact number appears naturally through bird behavior, clothing,
infrastructure, reflections, carried objects, or environmental use.

The pip plan is an acceptance contract, not loose inspiration. Use the
`numberSymbolism.element` field from the card data as the single countable pip
object for that card. Do not let the generator substitute alternate suit
objects, split the count across several object families, or add extra lookalike
props, reflections, lights, tags, feathers, bowls, birds, or clothing trim that
make the count ambiguous.

Pip elements should be:

- carried by beak, talons, feet, or legs;
- worn or clipped to bird-safe garments;
- guarded, arranged, built, shared, or moved by the bird;
- reflected in water, glass, wet pavement, or windows;
- embedded into practical infrastructure such as ledges, rails, crates,
  scaffolds, workbenches, canal lights, or antenna arrays;
- produced by the bird's posture, call, wingbeat, plumage display, gaze, or
  movement.

Pip elements must also remain inside the overlay-safe center. Any pip element
that will be cropped by a border rail, top medallion, corner ornament, or bottom
title cartouche fails the generation pass.
This check happens twice: once on the raw center art and again after the border
is composited. If a pip is present in the raw art but hidden by the final border,
regenerate the center art with the subject and pip cluster pulled inward.

Pip elements should not be:

- pasted into empty space;
- lined up as decorative icons with no scene role;
- loose count symbols floating around the bird;
- unrelated extra birds used only to reach a number;
- extra same-suit objects added as texture when they can be counted as pips;
- border motifs counted as scene pips;
- external props that look like the source of the card's action.

Before saving a generated Ace-Ten candidate, count the pip elements one by one.
Reject the candidate if the count is high, low, hidden by the frame, unclear, or
represented by the wrong object type. This is especially important for Basins
with repeated vessels/lights, Quills with feather-like pressure marks, Nests
with shine tags/charms, and Plumes with multiple birds or motion markers.

For Quills, reject Basin-like caregiver raincoats or soft care-station wardrobe
that make the card read as Basins. Quills should use signalwear, wind shells,
utility vests, courier layers, antenna/route infrastructure, pressure marks,
and rooftop strategy language.

When a card has a known generation failure, record the correction as
`repairDirective` in its canonical Minor Arcana JSON row. The prompt exporter
surfaces that directive in both full-card and center-art prompt packs. Treat the
directive as a hard reject gate for the next generation run, not as optional
style guidance.

Example planning corrections:

| Card | Weak Read | Stronger Read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nine of Quills | Nine loose quills glued around the bird | Nine pressure reflections across apartment windows and rain streaks caused by the bird's sleepless watch |
| Ten of Quills | A pile of many feathers | Ten pressure marks embedded into rooftop cracks and rain lines beside the recovering bird |
| Six of Plumes | Six electric rods staged under the bird | Six feather-shaped puddle reflections answering the bird's wingbeat and throat flash |
| Four of Nests | Four pasted coin icons | Four guarded shine tags built into a modern storage ledge |
| Ten of Basins | Ten random bowls | Ten canal-light basin markers integrated into a shelter scene |

## Suit Generation Notes

| Suit | Generation Emphasis | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plumes | Individualized performance streetwear, biological calls, wingbeats, throat flash, plumage color, iridescence, wet reflections, crowd attention, rhythm, and motion coming from the bird | Electrical beams, antenna-driven effects, spell particles, magic motes, black-and-bright gang-uniform palettes, identical performance outfits |
| Basins | Rainwear, care stations, canal shelter, first-aid pouches, soft utility gear, vessels and reflections used for healing or support | Plume stage energy, Quill signal arrays, Nest resource tags, old-world bathhouse fantasy |
| Quills | Modern signalwear, route logic, rooftop pressure, wind-cut marks, antenna infrastructure, messenger/courier/tactician fashion variety | Sci-fi armor, pasted loose feathers, Plume glow language, Nest workbench resource scenes |
| Nests | Hipster streetwear-workwear, flannel, beanies, chore coats, maker spaces, bike shops, co-ops, crates, scaffold resource logic | Medieval workshops, fantasy markets, Basin care vessels, Plume spectacle, Quill signal fields |

## Subagent Work Split

When using subagents for a regeneration round, split work by responsibility
rather than by taste:

| Role | Responsibility | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Border/template agent | Checks locked overlay geometry, title cartouche placement, output dimensions, and border consistency | Approved suit overlays and overlay QA notes |
| Prompt/pip agent | Writes card-specific center-art prompts and natural pip plans from card data | Prompt pack with exact pip briefs |
| Generation agent | Generates center art only, preserving anatomy, fashion, suit identity, and scene clarity | Candidate center-art images |
| Composite agent | Places center art under the locked overlay and renders deterministic title text | Final 1024x1536 card PNGs |
| QA agent | Reviews anatomy, species lock, suit isolation, pip count, border match, title text, and style fit | Pass/fail report and regeneration list |

Subagents should all reference `docs/art/art-bible.md` and this pipeline. No
subagent should invent a separate border system, suit vocabulary, title format,
or anatomy rule.

## Compositing Steps

1. Select the card data row: id, rank, suit, bird species, rarity, style
   direction, and description.
2. Write a pip plan for Ace through Ten, or a court-role plan for Fledgling,
   Outrider, Matron, and Elder.
3. Generate center art using the card-specific prompt.
4. Reject obvious failures before compositing: hands, arms, extra wings, extra
   legs, humanoid posture, mixed species, wrong suit, old-world fantasy,
   futuristic sci-fi, realistic/non-pixel art style, pasted pips, edge-cropped
   pips, subject heads or feet too close to the overlay, or gang-uniform
   styling.
5. Fit the center art into the locked image window without stretching the bird
   or cropping beak, feet, tail, or suit-defining action.
6. Place the locked suit border overlay above the center art.
7. Render deterministic title text into the bottom cartouche.
8. Export the final card as a 1024x1536 PNG.
9. Generate a contact sheet for review.
10. Record QA status and any regeneration notes.

## QA Gates

Each card must pass these gates before it can be considered accepted:

| Gate | Pass Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| Dimensions | Final PNG is exactly 1024x1536 |
| Border | Uses the accepted Tarot Aviary frame geometry and suit overlay |
| Title | Bottom cartouche contains the correct rank-and-suit title |
| Anatomy | Bird has natural avian body, exactly two wings, exactly two legs, no hands, no arms, no wing-hands, and no extra limbs |
| Species | Card depicts only the named species; no mixed flocks or hybrid traits |
| Suit Isolation | Card uses only its suit's motifs, props, setting, and behavior |
| Pip Count | Ace through Ten show the exact number through the card's named count element; no overcounts, undercounts, mixed count-object types, or ambiguous lookalike clutter |
| Pip Integration | Pip elements feel carried, worn, reflected, built, guarded, or produced by the bird's action |
| Bird-Originated Action | The card's effect reads as coming from the bird first, not from a prop or background device |
| Streetwear | Fashion is modern, individual, bird-safe, and not gang-coded |
| Plumes Resonance | Plumes light and sound read as biological or reflective, not electrical or technological |
| Text Hygiene | No readable clothing text, logos, slogans, crew names, brand marks, or pseudo-writing |

Any failure at the anatomy, border, title, species, suit isolation, pip count, or
dimension gate requires regeneration or recompositing before review continues.

## Expected Outputs

A complete generation round should produce:

- 56 data-derived locked SVG overlays using four shared suit frame treatments;
- a prompt pack with one prompt and one pip/court plan per Minor Arcana card;
- generated center-art candidates;
- final composited 1024x1536 card PNGs;
- a contact sheet per suit and one full Minor Arcana contact sheet;
- a QA summary listing accepted cards, rejected cards, and required rerolls.

The final exported cards should look like one coherent deck while allowing each
bird to keep its own species silhouette, outfit, personality, and scene logic.
