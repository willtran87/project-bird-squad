# Tarot Aviary Art Direction Contract

Version: 0.1

This document is a creative and production contract for a tarot-inspired bird game. It defines the starting art direction for the card set and should be treated as the shared source of truth for early concept art, prompt writing, asset review, and future gameplay prototyping.

## Core Rule

Every character remains anatomically bird.

Do not make the birds humanoid. Do not give them human torsos, arms, hands, human faces, human legs, or human posture. The body plan should stay avian: beak, skull, neck, feathered body, wings, tail feathers, bird legs, and taloned or webbed feet depending on species.

Modern street clothing must adapt to the bird, not reshape the bird into a person. Clothing can appear as fitted feather-safe garments, tiny jackets, wraps, hood shapes, talon bands, sneaker-like talon covers, harness pouches, chains, pins, patches, scarves, caps, or accessories.

## Visual Pillars

- High-resolution pixel art with crisp clusters, deliberate dithering, and readable silhouettes.
- Tarot card composition with full-body subject focus, ornate borders, symbolic framing, and a clear title area.
- Modern urban fantasy tone: streetwear, neon accents, night city texture, worn signage, mystical symbols, and ritual objects.
- The mood should be stylish, strange, sincere, and iconic rather than cute parody.
- Each card should be strong enough to guide a playable character, faction, or combat archetype later.

## Anatomy Rules

- Birds must retain natural species-specific proportions.
- Wings are wings, not arms.
- Feet are talons or webbed bird feet, not shoes on human feet.
- Accessories can wrap, hang, clip, or sit on the bird's natural form.
- Expressions come from eye shape, head angle, feather posture, beak angle, pose, and lighting.
- Avoid human hand gestures, bipedal superhero poses, human shoulders, human hips, and upright human fashion-model stance.

## Tarot Card Rules

- Each card should preserve the tarot archetype while reinterpreting it through bird behavior, habitat, movement, and symbolism.
- Dark cards should be mystical and transformative, not gore-focused.
- The Hanged Man should use natural inverted bird behavior or peaceful levitation. No rope, no noose, no hanging device, no harm imagery.
- Card titles use classic Major Arcana names unless later renamed by the game.
- Text should be minimal and readable. Prefer title only.

## Shared Image Prompt Template

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: high-resolution tarot card illustration
Primary request: A tarot card inspired by [CARD], reimagined as an anatomically correct [BIRD] in modern streetwear details.
Scene/backdrop: ornate tarot-card frame with subtle urban fantasy details, symbolic objects tied to the card meaning, and a clean card border.
Subject: a fully anatomically avian [BIRD]. Natural beak, head, neck, feathered body, wings, tail feathers, bird legs, and taloned or webbed feet. No human torso, arms, hands, human face, human legs, or humanoid posture.
Streetwear adaptation: [GARMENTS AND ACCESSORIES] fitted to bird anatomy.
Style/medium: high-resolution pixel art, crisp pixel clusters, detailed dithering, polished game-art quality.
Composition/framing: vertical portrait tarot card, centered full-body bird, clear silhouette, decorative border, readable card title at bottom.
Lighting/mood: [MOOD AND LIGHTING]
Color palette: [PALETTE]
Text (verbatim): "[CARD TITLE]"
Constraints: anatomically bird, not humanoid, no watermark, no extra text.
Avoid: human anatomy, hands, human face, human limbs, blurry pixel art, generic fantasy costume.
```

## First Production Slice

Start with the Major Arcana before expanding into suits. These 22 cards are enough to define the game's visual language, character archetypes, symbolic palette, and early mechanics.

Suggested first concept set:

- The Fool: establishes tone and protagonist energy.
- The Magician: establishes magic and tool language.
- The High Priestess: establishes mystery and lore.
- The Chariot: establishes motion and combat energy.
- The Hanged Man: establishes the non-humanoid anatomy rule under a difficult tarot archetype.
- Death: establishes how dark cards are handled without gore.
- The Star: establishes hope, healing, and beauty.
- The World: establishes endgame scale.

## Review Checklist

- Is the bird anatomically bird?
- Does the species choice support the tarot meaning?
- Is the streetwear fitted to bird anatomy?
- Is the silhouette readable at card scale?
- Does the card feel like pixel art, not a blurred painting with pixel artifacts?
- Is the title readable and limited to the intended card name?
- Are symbolic elements present without cluttering the card?
- Does the card avoid human anatomy, human hands, gore, ropes, nooses, and watermark text?
