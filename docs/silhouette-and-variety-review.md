# Silhouette & Variety Review

Version: 0.1 (companion to `tarot-aviary-art-direction.md`)

Purpose: a concept-artist-facing diagnostic for the 22 Major Arcana. It maps where
the deck repeats itself on the three variety levers that matter inside a single
unified style — **silhouette**, **pose energy**, and **palette** — and gives a
per-cluster instruction for keeping cards distinct at card scale.

Species variety is already strong and is not the concern. The concern is that
several distinct species share a body-plan, so they can blur at thumbnail / in-hand
scale unless deliberately separated. This directly serves the existing visual
pillar: **readable silhouettes**.

---

## 1. Silhouette clusters (the clash matrix)

Each row is a group of cards whose birds share a body-plan. "Clash risk" is how
likely two cards in the group read as the same shape at small scale, before color.

| Silhouette class | Cards (bird) | Clash risk | How to separate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small perching songbird | Fool (House Sparrow), Hanged Man (Nuthatch), Star (Superb Fairywren), Sun (American Goldfinch) | **High** | Vary by *pose + scale-in-frame*: Fool airborne mid-leap, Nuthatch inverted, Fairywren tiny under a huge sky, Goldfinch open-wing flight. No two share the same head angle. |
| Perched raptor | Emperor (Golden Eagle), Chariot (Peregrine Falcon), High Priestess (Barn Owl), Hermit (Snowy Owl), Death (Turkey Vulture) | **High** | Separate by *posture + framing*: Emperor monumental/folded, Chariot committed dive, Death hunched-grounded or circling. Keep the two owls apart (see below). |
| Owl (subset of above) | High Priestess (Barn Owl), Hermit (Snowy Owl) | **High** | Barn Owl = tight crop, heart-shaped face, veil hood. Snowy Owl = full-body, round white mass, heavy cloak. Different crop, different value (dark-framed vs bright). |
| Black corvid | Magician (Magpie), Devil (Common Raven) | **Medium** | Magpie keeps strong black/white pied contrast + tool clutter. Raven is all-black glossy, sparse, luxe. Already distinct via color; hold the line on it. |
| Tall long-legged wader | Hierophant (Sandhill Crane), Justice (Black-Necked Stilt), Temperance (Flamingo), Strength (Secretarybird) | **Medium-High** | Crane = vertical neck, calm. Stilt = ultra-thin symmetrical. Flamingo = one-leg curve, pink. Secretarybird = crest + action pose. Lean on palette + crest/neck shape. |
| Glossy dark body (cross-class) | Devil (Raven), Death (Turkey Vulture) | **Medium** | Both dark and brooding. Death uses rust/pale-dawn warmth behind it; Devil uses oil-slick purple + silver. Never put both on a flat black field. |

Cards outside any cluster (already distinctive silhouettes): Empress (swan),
Lovers (paired birds), Wheel (kingfisher), Tower (clinging woodpecker),
Moon (cryptic ground nightjar), Judgment (rooster), World (tern in wreath).

## 2. Pose energy distribution

The current deck skews heavily static. Roughly 15 of 22 read as a calm perch or
standing stance; the words "natural / calm / still" recur. Target a rhythm closer
to a 40 / 35 / 25 split so the deck has motion when fanned out.

| Energy | Definition | Current cards | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Static | Perched / standing / looking forward | ~15 | ~9 |
| Active | Clear directional intent, wings or legs engaged, not airborne | ~4 | ~8 |
| Dynamic | Airborne, diving, mid-action, or a genuinely unusual pose | ~3 | ~5 |

Cards to push **up** in energy (cheap wins that stay species-natural):

- **The Fool** — commit the takeoff: airborne mid-leap off the curb, not a hop.
- **Strength (Secretarybird)** — a *restrained* mid-stomp (its real hunting kick) sells "controlled power" far better than a calm stand.
- **Wheel of Fortune (Kingfisher)** — a committed plunge-dive, not a perch option.
- **Judgment (Rooster)** — full open-wing crow, lifted and loud.
- **The Tower (Woodpecker)** — clinging *through* impact: sparks, a cracking sign, mid-hammer.

Keep static (it suits the archetype): High Priestess, Hermit, Empress,
Hierophant, Justice, Moon, Devil. Stillness is the point on these.

## 3. Palette clusters

Two formulas repeat. They are fine in moderation but currently over-used.

**Monochrome + neon accent (black/white/silver dominant):** Magician, Justice,
Devil, Death, Moon. Keep, but make sure each has a *different* accent hue so they
don't become one black-and-white block.

**Cool nocturnal blue/silver:** High Priestess, Hermit, Star, Moon, World — five
cards in nearly the same cold register. Break them apart with one distinct
secondary accent each:

| Card | Keep (cool base) | Add distinct accent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High Priestess | indigo / moon white | aged gold (royal-mystic) |
| Hermit | ice blue / navy | warm lantern amber (single warm point of light) |
| Star | electric blue / lavender | soft rose / peach (dawn-of-hope glow) |
| Moon | charcoal / muted blue | sickly muted green (eerie, uneasy) |
| World | sea blue / silver | aurora green (already its differentiator — lean in) |

Warm cards are well distributed and need no change: Fool, Sun, Empress, Lovers,
Temperance, Judgment, Wheel.

---

## 4. One-line instruction for the artist

> Species are already varied. Your job at card scale is to keep **shape, energy,
> and accent hue** distinct — especially among the small songbirds, the raptors
> (and the two owls), the tall waders, and the five cool-blue mystical cards.
